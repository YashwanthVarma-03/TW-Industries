What a confirmed Moving work order looks like

"Confirmed work order" is not marketing language - it is a specific thing. Here is a real one, start to finish.

A homeowner in your ZIP has a lease ending on the 31st and needs to move to a new apartment across town. She opens the marketplace, filters to Moving, sees your company - registered, insured, 4.9 rating - with upfront hourly pricing, and books a two-mover crew for that morning. She approves the rate and minimum when she books.

The order drops into your queue already complete: the service (Local Moving), the hourly rate and minimum, both addresses, the move date, and notes that it's a two-bedroom apartment on the third floor with an elevator and a few heavy items. You accept it. No one else in your ZIP can take it.

Two demand sources, one membership

Your single moving membership is fed by two different kinds of customer, and the second one brings the steady, repeatable relocation volume that smooths out a seasonal trade.

Homeowners - The classic residential mix - local apartment, condo, and house moves priced by crew and hours, packing and unpacking services, labor-only loading and unloading, and single-item furniture delivery. Demand peaks in summer and at every month's end when leases turn over. They find you through the marketplace, pick you on your pricing, registration, and rating, and book directly.

Property managers, senior living & commercial - This is the volume most lead platforms cannot give you, and for movers it is the steadying force: apartment and condo turnovers, senior-living and assisted-living move-ins, corporate and office relocations, and staging moves for realtors. Property managers and communities generate a constant stream of resident moves against written service levels (routine within 48 hours, urgent within 24, emergency within 4). One recurring senior-living or property-management relationship can fill the slow weeks that would otherwise sit empty between peak-season moves.

Moving contractor questions

How do moving companies get work orders through TradeWorks Select?

Apply once and get vetted. After approval, property managers, senior communities, businesses, and homeowners in your ZIP browse the network, see upfront pricing, and book a move on your calendar. The order arrives confirmed - date, scope, and access already set - and you are the only company on it, not one of six on a shared lead.

How much does a TradeWorks Select moving membership cost?

One flat monthly membership, priced to your ZIP's market. Across moving in Florida the median is around $120 per month, but your exact figure depends on your ZIP - enter it in the lookup on this page. There are no per-job fees, no percentages, and no cost when a job cancels. It is introductory launch pricing and early members lock in their rate.

Why isn't the price a flat national number?

Because a ZIP's demand, home values, and cost to serve are not the same everywhere, a single national price would overcharge some companies and undercharge others. TradeWorks prices each ZIP to its own market from a statewide model, so the number is fair and defensible - and you can see it before you apply.

Is this a moving lead generation service?

No - and that's the whole point. Moving leads are sold to five or six companies who then underbid each other for a job the customer may never book. Select sends confirmed work orders that a customer has already booked - one company per order, no bidding, no shared leads.

What's the best Thumbtack, Angi, or HomeAdvisor alternative for movers?

TradeWorks Select. Those platforms charge per shared lead whether or not it converts, and hand the same move to several companies at once. Select charges one flat membership, every order is already booked, and you're the only company on it. You keep 100% of the invoice, and a cancelled order costs you nothing extra.

How can I get moving jobs without buying shared leads?

Join a network that sends confirmed work instead of selling contact information. With TradeWorks Select, homeowners, property managers, and businesses book you directly through the marketplace, so every job is already scoped and scheduled - nothing to chase, no per-lead fees, no bidding war, and one flat monthly membership as your only cost.

Do I need to be licensed or registered to join as a mover?

Yes. In Florida, intrastate household-goods movers must be registered with FDACS under Chapter 507; interstate movers need USDOT/FMCSA registration. We verify that registration plus your insurance - $1M/$2M general liability naming TradeWorks AI as additional insured, cargo/valuation coverage, and workers' comp if you have employees. Verified registration is part of what makes the network trustworthy to customers.

How many moving companies do you accept per ZIP code?

Seats per ZIP are deliberately limited to protect work-order volume and pricing for the members already in the network. The exact number a ZIP supports is shown in the lookup on this page. Once a ZIP is full, applications for it close until a seat opens.
