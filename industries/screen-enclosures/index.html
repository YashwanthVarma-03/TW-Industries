What a confirmed Screen Enclosure work order looks like

"Confirmed work order" is not marketing language - it is a specific thing, and it comes in two flavors depending on the job. Here is how both work.

First, a rescreen. A homeowner in your ZIP has a pool cage with torn, sagging screens after a storm. She opens the marketplace, filters to Screen Enclosures, sees your posted rescreen rate and a 4.9 rating, and books for Thursday. The order lands in your queue with the scope, the address, and the time - booked at your rate. You rescreen the cage, check the frame while you're there, and invoice her directly. That's a Rate Card job.

Second, a new pool cage. A homeowner with a new pool wants a full enclosure. He books an assessment. Because a pool cage is an engineered aluminum structure that has to meet Florida Building Code wind-load standards, you measure the pool and deck, handle the engineering and permit, and provide a quote he approves before work starts. That's a Quote Request job - the higher-value, engineered work.
