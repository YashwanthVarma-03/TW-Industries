What a confirmed Pest Control work order looks like

"Confirmed work order" is not marketing language - it is a specific thing, and for pest control it often turns into a recurring customer. Here is a real one, start to finish.

A homeowner in your ZIP starts seeing ants marching across the kitchen counter and a few palmetto bugs at night. She opens the marketplace, filters to Pest Control, sees your company with an upfront price and a 4.9 rating, and books an initial general pest treatment for Thursday, 10 AM to noon. She approves the price when she books.

The order drops into your queue already complete: the service (General Pest Control), the agreed price, the address, the two-hour window, and a note about the ant trails and a dog in the home. You accept it. No one else in your ZIP can take it.

Two demand sources, one membership

Your single pest control membership is fed by two different kinds of customer, and for this trade both of them lean recurring - which is exactly what you want.

Homeowners - The classic residential mix - general pest and recurring quarterly plans, seasonal mosquito and rodent surges, bed-bug and wildlife jobs, and the higher-ticket termite and WDO work that comes with home sales. They find you through the marketplace, pick you on your pricing and rating, book directly, and - treated well - stay on a plan for years.

Property managers - This is the volume most lead platforms cannot give you, and pest control is one of the trades property managers rely on most. They run dozens of doors - single-family rentals, small multifamily, HOAs - and nearly all of them carry a recurring pest agreement, plus turnover treatments between tenants, bed-bug remediation, and WDO inspections at sale. Through TradeWorks they book against written service levels (routine within 48 hours, urgent within 24, emergency within 4) and come back like clockwork. For a pest control company, that is recurring, multi-property revenue that compounds - the most valuable book of business in the trade.
