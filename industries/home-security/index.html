What a confirmed Home Security work order looks like

"Confirmed work order" is not marketing language - it is a specific thing. Here is a real one, start to finish.

A homeowner in your ZIP wants cameras and an alarm system before the family travels for the summer. Because she's trusting the installer with the security of her home, she's looking for someone vetted, not an anonymous name from a lead form. She opens the marketplace, filters to Home Security, sees your company - licensed, background-checked, 4.9 rating - and books an assessment for Wednesday.

The order drops into your queue already complete: the service (Security & Alarm Install), the entry points and camera locations she has in mind, the address, the time window, and a note that it's a whole-home system. Because it's larger, it's quoted through the closed process with you and at most one other vetted pro - not five. You accept it.

Two demand sources, one membership

Your single home security membership is fed by two different kinds of customer, and both come with recurring monitoring revenue on top of the install.

Homeowners - The classic residential mix - alarm systems, security cameras, video doorbells, smart locks and smart-home setups, and system repair. In Florida, seasonal residents drive a huge amount of remote-monitoring demand - snowbirds leave homes empty for months and want to watch them from up north - and summer travel and the holidays add more. Homeowners choose a security company on trust above all. They find you through the marketplace, pick you on your rating and credentials, and book real work.

Property managers & commercial - This is the volume most lead platforms cannot give you, and for security companies it's steady recurring work: multi-family and apartment security and access control, community and HOA camera systems, commercial cameras and alarms, and vacation-rental monitoring across a portfolio. Property managers coordinate big, repeatable installs and ongoing monitoring, booked against written service levels (routine within 48 hours, urgent within 24, emergency within 4). A single commercial or multi-family system, plus its monitoring, can be worth many residential jobs.
