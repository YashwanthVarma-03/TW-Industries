What a confirmed Cleaning work order looks like

"Confirmed work order" is not marketing language - it is a specific thing, and for cleaning it often turns into a recurring client. Here is a real one, start to finish.

A homeowner in your ZIP is overwhelmed and wants regular help keeping the house clean. She opens the marketplace, filters to Cleaning, sees your company - background-checked, bonded, and insured, with a 4.9 rating and upfront pricing - and books a first deep clean for Thursday, 9 AM to noon. She approves the price when she books.

The order drops into your queue already complete: the service (Deep Cleaning), the agreed price, the address, the time window, and a note about two dogs and a lockbox code for entry. You accept it. No one else in your ZIP can take it.

Two demand sources, one membership

Your single cleaning membership is fed by two different kinds of customer, and for this trade both of them lean recurring - which is exactly what you want.

Homeowners - The classic residential mix - recurring weekly and biweekly house cleaning, one-time deep cleans, move-in and move-out cleaning, and specialty work like carpet, tile-and-grout, upholstery, and windows. They find you through the marketplace, pick you on your pricing, reviews, and the trust of a background-checked, insured company, and - treated well - stay on a plan for years.

Property managers - This is the volume most lead platforms cannot give you, and for cleaning it is enormous. Property managers run dozens of doors and need make-ready and move-out cleans on every turnover, recurring common-area cleaning, and - across Florida's vacation-rental market - fast turnover cleans between guests. Through TradeWorks they book against written service levels (routine within 48 hours, urgent within 24, emergency within 4) and come back constantly. For a cleaning company, turnover and recurring commercial work is steady, repeatable revenue that compounds - the most valuable book of business in the trade.
